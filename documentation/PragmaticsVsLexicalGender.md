# Pragmatic Vs Lexical Gender
Ahuzacos differs from Francais in that it has both Japanese pragmatic gender and French lexical gender. This was one of the reasons I invented pseudo lexical gender for Japanese loan words.

Therefore certain words gender lexical gender based on the context of who is speaking or what person possesses a thing, where the pragmatic gender overrides the natural lexical gender.

## Pragmatic Gender
Ahuzacos is organized by standard Japanese honorifics, that are further split into masculine, feminine, and neuter ( neuter itself can be split between masculine and neuter. )

This does not always align with the lexical gender of a French term, and sometimes you have to find a compromise between French Lexical gender, and assumed lexical gender in the Japanese equivalent.

## Lexical Gender
Lexical gender originally aligned with French lexical gender and for the most part continues to do so, but this runs into issues when dealing with loan words from Japanese that have no lexical gender. While fruits themselves are assumed to be feminine, Japanese historically has no such taxonomy system. So you'd have to guess what gender a thing has in contexts where such information is not available.

Thus for practical reasons that means that pomme is moved toward being Masculine gender, while apuru is assumed to be feminine in gender.
